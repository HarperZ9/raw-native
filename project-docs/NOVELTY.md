# Effects program: novelty record

Dated 2026-10-15. This file backs the word "novel" in EFFECTS-PROGRAM.md. A candidate stays "candidate" until a row here says otherwise. Shader lab 2 keeps its own record for its five techniques (kept outside this repository); this file covers the graphics program only.

Limits, stated once and applying to every row:
- Searches were general web searches (eight on 2026-10-15). Google Scholar, ACM DL, IEEE Xplore, GDC Vault and Shadertoy were not queried (lab 2 found the ACM DL and Shadertoy behind bot checks on 2026-10-10; that has not changed here).
- Result pages were read as summaries, not as full texts.
- "No prior found" means a limited search found none. It is not proof of novelty. Public wording stays "new to us" until a direct index search confirms.

## Checked on 2026-10-15

| Technique (as built or proposed) | Verdict | Nearest prior found | What ours adds, and the check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dither-quantised lighting: light and shadow resolved through an ordered threshold matrix into N tones, 1 bit as the limit case (built, web/shaders/ditherlight) | KNOWN | A Godot 2D "Retro Dithered Lighting and Fog-of-War" shader (smoothstep radial falloff per light, summed, then a Bayer 8x8 threshold and level quantisation). A Godot 1-bit spatial shader whose author notes the quantisation must run after lighting. Return of the Obra Dinn (Lucas Pope, 2018) and its open-source imitations. | Nothing claimed as new. What we add is engineering and measurement: a linear-light fraction so the dither is mean-preserving to half a rank (tested), shaped beam masks with blades, fog to paper white, a G-buffer path so RT shadow rays can feed visibility, CPU and GPU parity, and a motion study. |
| Pattern anchored to the surface with a near-constant on-screen dot size (world-octave option, built) | KNOWN | Dither3D, "Surface-Stable Fractal Dithering", Rune Skovbo Johansen (runevision.com/tech/dither3d, open source): dots stick to surfaces and are added or removed so size stays constant on screen; the added constraint is that shrinking removes dots without adding any. Obra Dinn's sphere-mapped dither (pinned during rotation, not translation). | Ours snaps the cell size by powers of two per pixel from depth. It has seams that Dither3D's fractal construction avoids (seam fraction 1.7% on a dolly) and it costs accuracy (see DITHER-LIGHTING-RESULTS.md). We do not claim it improves on Dither3D. The measured result that screen anchoring wins on accuracy is a finding about our implementation. |
| Bayer against blue noise under motion | KNOWN as a discussion | Pope's devlog (TIGSource, summarised by several articles): Bayer reproduces tone more accurately, blue noise looks more organic and survives scaling and compression better. | Our numbers agree on accuracy (Bayer about 45% lower box-4 error) and add that the two are not separated on temporal metrics. One scene, software metrics only. |
| Paint-over projection: paint over a camera-locked render of block-out geometry and project it back as the surface (built, web/shaders/paintover) | KNOWN | Camera projection mapping and 2.5D matte painting (SideFX forum threads; Texas A&M thesis on camera-based texture mapping). Blending several projected images by the angle to the viewer is view-dependent texture mapping: Debevec, Taylor and Malik, SIGGRAPH 1996 (Facade), and Debevec, Yu and Borshukov 1998 (view maps, projective texturing). | Nothing claimed as new. Our additions are engineering and measurement: windows that reach to the nearest other projector so every keyframe view is exact (max 0 codes over three projectors), a copy-then-fill secondary painting, CPU and GPU parity, and an off-axis study of stretch and holes. |
| Greedy suggestion of secondary projectors from the points the primary cannot reach (built) | KNOWN | View selection as a set-cover problem solved greedily for texturing (TexRO, arXiv 2403.15009); next-best-view planning (Pito 1999 and later); "PaintersView: Automatic Suggestion of Optimal Viewpoints for 3D Texture Painting" (title seen only). | Nothing claimed. The 2.5D depth-map projector setting is a narrower case the search did not find covered, which is not evidence of novelty. |
| Palette-locked lighting: light resolved into palette ramps, with palette-cycle animation | KNOWN as pieces | Godot shaders for palette-ordered dithering and 3D post palette modes. A forum workflow that picks the nearest palette colour after adding noise. The same Godot 1-bit shader notes quantising after lighting. | Hypothesis only: lighting evaluated in the palette's own ramp coordinates (hue-shifted ramps from a spectral light) so a lamp changes hue along authored ramps, not just value. Not found in this search. "Candidate". |
| Film halation (red-first), gate weave, emulsion grain | KNOWN | Deep Analog (arXiv 2608.14702, per-channel halation blur radii at 1.4 : 1.0 : 0.7, set empirically); Digital Production's halation article (red layer deepest, warm colour from emulsion structure); Dehancer and Kdenlive gate weave; shader lab 1 in this repository already has a spectral halation and gate weave. | Nothing new claimed for these three. |
| Palette-locked lighting: light resolved into hue-shifted palette ramps, dithered by Bayer thresholds, with a warm fraction choosing the ramp variant, and palette cycling for water (built, web/shaders/palettelight) | KNOWN as pieces | Pixel-art practice of hue-shifted ramps (cool shadows, warm lights; Pixelblog and gamedev.net palette articles); lighting as an index into an ordered ramp with dither between steps (Godot palette and dither shaders); colour cycling for water and clouds (Mark Ferrari for LucasArts; BlendShift cycling; Joe Huckaby's HTML5 engine; a 2024 paper on generating palette-cycling animation from video). | Nothing claimed. The one piece the search did not find stated is choosing between a cool and a warm ramp per pixel by dithering the warm fraction of the irradiance, so a lamp changes hue and not only value. It is a small step and stays candidate. |

## Checked on 2026-10-15 (second search: lens suite, RT visibility, Low Water candidates)

The searches were general web, Google Scholar and arXiv. The ACM Digital Library and Shadertoy were tried directly, through fetch and through a real browser. Both returned a bot-verification page every time (fetch: HTTP 403 on dl.acm.org search and DOI pages and on the Shadertoy search, API and view pages; browser: a security-verification interstitial on dl.acm.org and shadertoy.com that did not clear). I did not try to get past those checks. Semantic Scholar returned 429 and dblp a bot wall. Shadertoy coverage is therefore unknown, and so is ACM coverage beyond what mirrors index (the Eurographics digital library, the MPI project page, SIGGRAPH History).

|---|---|---|---|
| Lens ghosts from a prescription by paraxial matrices, glass dispersion, thin-film coating tint (built, web/shaders/lens) | KNOWN | Hullin, Eisemann, Seidel and Lee, "Physically-based real-time lens flare rendering", SIGGRAPH 2011 (aberrations and anti-reflective coatings); Lee and Eisemann, "Practical real-time lens-flare rendering", EGSR 2013 (first-order paraxial matrices); Pekkarinen and Balzer, DigiPro 2019 (The Lego Movie 2) | Nothing claimed. Our additions are measurement: an exact meridional trace that checks every paraxial ghost (0.0008 px at 0.2 degrees) and shows where the paraxial regime breaks (121 px at 10 degrees). |
| Iris diffraction starburst by FFT of an N-blade aperture, per wavelength (built) | KNOWN | Ritschel et al., "Temporal Glare", Eurographics 2009; Hullin 2011; a gamedev.net blog of a per-frame Fraunhofer spectrum of the aperture | Nothing claimed. |
| One thin-film coating spectrum that tints both the ghosts and the anamorphic streak (built) | CANDIDATE | Hullin's coating model tints ghosts only. Streak tints in tools are set by hand. | A single physical source for both tints. A small step. Not found in this search, and both main indexes were unreachable. |
| An energy-closed lens stage: source tiles give up exactly what ghosts, streak, starburst and halation add (built) | CANDIDATE, partial prior | A forum argument that bloom should scatter light and not add it (Polycount). An energy-conserving bloom in a Call of Duty talk, cited from memory with low confidence. | The closed ledger across all terms, measured at 0.002%. |
| Lens controls keyed to time-of-day slots, with the f-number coupling ghost size and spike length (built) | CANDIDATE, design | Standard optics. | A design choice, not a research result. |
| One ray per pixel, light sample from the pixel's Bayer rank through a (0,6,3)-net, so the dither threshold also stratifies visibility (built, PR #152) | CANDIDATE as an idea; NEGATIVE as measured | Georgiev and Fajardo 2016 (blue-noise dithered sampling); Heitz and Belcour 2019; Heitz et al. 2019; Belcour and Heitz 2021. None of these uses a Bayer matrix as both the quantiser and the sampler. | Unbiased and flicker-free, but no more accurate than one white-noise ray. It is recorded as a negative result, not as a contribution. |
| Tide-memory wetness: wetness decays from the time since the water last covered a surface (proposed) | PARTIAL | Jensen, Legakis and Dorsey, "Rendering of Wet Materials", EGWR 1999 (the look, not drying over time). A drying-materials paper hosted at Yale whose authors were not confirmed. Alan Wake 2's height-buffer rain wetness. | The tidal history as the state. Unchecked in game devlogs, which are poorly indexed. |
| Paint-over projection plus a traced view-dependent reflection layer (proposed) | CANDIDATE | 2.5D matte painting and camera projection (SideFX forum); Debevec 1996 view-dependent texturing; a patent on projection-painting preview (US 8,373,715). | The runtime reflection pass over a projected painting. |
| Low-pressure sodium as a near-monochromatic 589 nm light in a game renderer (proposed) | CANDIDATE | The physics (RP Photonics on sodium lamps); remarks that RGB rendering cannot reproduce it (Moments in Graphics, spectral rendering series). | Colour that vanishes only under sodium light, as a game mechanic. |
